Reading topological results

Topology describes connectivity and holes. A finite sample does not arrive with a unique topology: we choose a distance, neighborhood construction and scale convention first. These choices express the scientific question.

Four objects that should stay distinct

ObjectWhat it recordsTypical JuliaTDA representation
Point cloudOriginal observations and geometryEuclideanSpace, vector of points
Simplicial or cubical complexCells and how their boundaries attachRips, cubical or custom filtration
Persistence diagramBirth/death intervals of homology classesPersistenceDiagram
Mapper graphOverlap between clusters in a chosen coverM.g together with M.C and M.X

The same point cloud can produce all four. A loop in a Mapper graph and an H₁ persistence interval are outcomes of different constructions and need not agree. A graph also has its own cycles: they should not be confused with cycles of the cloud's Rips complex.

A square explains a filtration

Take the four vertices of a unit square. At Rips distance 0, they are four components. At distance 1, its side edges appear and form a loop. At distance √2, the diagonals and triangles appear, filling that loop. Its ideal H₁ interval is (1, √2).

using JuliaTDA
square = [[0.0, 0.0], [1.0, 0.0], [1.0, 1.0], [0.0, 1.0]]
square_diagrams = ripserer(square; dim_max = 1)
[(birth(interval), death(interval)) for interval in square_diagrams[2]]
1-element Vector{Tuple{Float64, Float64}}:
 (1.0, 1.4142135623730951)

In a Rips filtration, a simplex enters when all its edges do; the scale is the largest pairwise distance. It is not the radius of a drawn disk. Other filtrations use other axes: intensity in a grayscale image, directional height for an Euler transform, or a scalar measurement on vertices. Always label the axis with the actual quantity and units.

An H₀ class often survives forever because the final complex stays connected. With a finite computation threshold, a class with infinite death can also be right-censored: its death may occur beyond the observed range. Check the filtration and threshold before calling it a permanent hole. Vectorizations and distances have their own policies for essential intervals; use compatible policies across all samples.

Why persistence is useful, and what it does not guarantee

Persistence organizes features by lifetime. Under appropriate assumptions, small perturbations of a filtration lead to small changes in its diagram. This motivates sensitivity checks. It does not select a correct distance, guarantee recovery from sparse sampling, or calibrate a statistical test.

A long interval may reflect a real cycle, a geometric outlier, anisotropic units, or preprocessing choices. Compare multiple samples or plausible null models, not only one attractive plot. Diagram distances also differ: a bottleneck distance emphasizes the worst matched discrepancy, while a Wasserstein distance aggregates discrepancies with its chosen exponent.

Mapper: cover, cluster, connect

A Mapper construction has three stages:

  1. Cover the range of a filter with overlapping sets and pull them back to observations. A filter can be a coordinate, density, eccentricity, or a learned measurement.
  2. Split each pulled-back set into clusters using distances in the original space. Two separated arcs of a circle at the same x-coordinate should be distinct clusters when the radius permits it.
  3. Connect resulting clusters that share observations, or use the explicitly chosen alternative nerve rule.

Filter values guide the cover; they need not be the metric used for clustering. More cover intervals resolve finer filter changes but can make the result fragmented. More overlap can add connections. A larger clustering radius can merge separated structures. Vary these settings together, keeping the data representation and metric fixed when comparing outcomes.

Ball Mapper instead covers the original metric space with balls around landmarks. Its parameters have distance units. With an insufficient radius, some observations may have no membership. With too much overlap, a dense graph can obscure structure. Inspect membership and coverage explicitly.

Density modes are another kind of topology

ToMATo follows density peaks on a proximity graph and merges shallow peaks into deeper ones. Its merge threshold is in density units, not geometric distance units. Replacing the density estimator or changing its bandwidth changes that scale, so the same numerical threshold cannot be assumed to mean the same thing.

The graph determines which modes can meet. Disconnected graph components cannot merge, regardless of a large threshold. Essential surviving peaks use the package's death = Inf sentinel; it does not mean an infinitely large density drop. See the ToMATo package's interpretation guide for the exact merge inequality and handling of tied densities.

Two parameters do not produce one barcode

A function-Rips bifiltration asks for both a distance scale and a vertex measurement cutoff. An inclusion is possible when both coordinates increase. Such a partially ordered family generally has no ordinary barcode that contains all its information.

The bundled MultiparameterPersistence package supplies useful summaries for small complexes: a Hilbert function counts homology at each grade; grid Koszul Betti numbers record generators, relations and syzygies of the sampled module; an Euler surface is an alternating cell or homology count. These quantities are related but are not interchangeable. A signed Euler measure can cancel contributions, so a zero measure need not describe an empty complex.

A finite grid is part of the model. Missing critical grades loses information. Stability statements for smooth signed-measure features require their stated matching and fixed-combinatorics assumptions; they do not make raw grid Betti numbers universally stable.

What counts as an observation in inference?

A point cloud has observations within a shape. A collection of diagrams has one observation per sampled shape. Resampling points from one cloud, constructing a mean landscape band across independent diagrams, and permuting paired diagram labels address different sampling models.

Overlapping time windows, repeated measurements of the same subject, and multiple nodes containing the same point introduce dependence. A function accepting these arrays does not make an independence assumption true. Define the experimental unit and exchangeability scheme before selecting a method.
