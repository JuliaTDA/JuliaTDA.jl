# Roadmap verification — 25 November 2026

The local ecosystem implements P4.1–P4.7 and the companion performance
checklist. Run the experiments using the [benchmark commands](../../benchmarks/performance/README.md).
The [raw measurements](2026-11-25-roadmap-data/) include fixture hashes,
source revisions and working-tree fingerprints, package versions, individual
samples, cold and warm wall time, and peak process RSS. The torus and 32³ volume
were also repeated with per-sample peer validation after the runner update. The main results are
medians of three warm runs on an Intel Core i9-13980HX with Julia 1.12.5 and one
BLAS thread. Reported times varied across runs on this machine.

## P4 acceptance

| Item | Verification |
| --- | --- |
| P4.1 edge collapse | Barcode parity on dense and sparse matrices with zero edges, nonzero vertex births, thresholds and ties. Edge state and weights are indexed by graph edges. |
| P4.2 indexed Rips | Inclusive thresholds tested at the adjacent Float32 and Float64 values around a known edge grade. The density sweep checked 48 clouds and thresholds against both explicit paths. |
| P4.3 grid invariants | Full Euler and H₀–H₂ grids agree with direct reference evaluation, including irregular grades, ties, empty grades and a fixture with a surviving cycle. |
| P4.4 RedZeD | Endpoint and simplex-grade checks cover dense Float32/Float64 F₂ Rips, with separate dense and sparse cohomology arms on the shared fixtures. The vendored code retains the MIT copyright notice. |
| P4.5 cubical union-find | Reference tests cover tied grades, threshold censoring, exterior faces and thin grids. Larger images and volumes match every CubicalRipser endpoint, using the same vertex-valued convention. |
| P4.6 zigzag representatives | An independent dense oracle checks every subinterval rank, active simplices, cycle boundaries, inclusion maps and basis ranks. Representatives are opt-in via `reps=true`. |
| P4.7 AdaMapper | A circle fixture recovers a loop missed by the coarse cover. A branched fixture agrees with the regular cover. Tests also cover the relative-persistence cutoff, constant lenses and bad arguments. |

The 24 × 24 multiparameter fixture took 0.24 ms for H₀, 0.51 ms for H₁ and
0.75 ms for H₂. Evaluating independent grid points through the reference
homology routine took 57, 202 and 398 ms respectively. Euler prefix sums took
0.076 ms versus 0.267 ms for direct counting.

## Cross-framework persistence

Rips arms use F₂ and all barcodes through the indicated dimension. Shared
matrices have Float32 distances. The torus also tests independent Float64
construction from the source points. Torus barcodes agree after converting
those endpoints to Float32. The Julia backends, non-collapsed giotto-ph, and CubicalRipser arms
matched the relevant references exactly, including the multiplicity of
essential bars. An O(3) repeat exposed an intermittent mismatch in giotto-ph
0.2.4 with edge collapse and eight threads. A follow-up matched 20 of 20
samples with one thread and 19 of 20 with eight. Its eight-thread timings
are excluded from the exact comparison below. The
[endpoint deltas](2026-11-25-roadmap-data/giotto-collapse-stability.json)
include a missing essential H₁ interval from the first run and an extra
essential H₂ interval from the follow-up.

The collapse profiles attribute much of the sampled O(3) execution to
neighborhood dominance checks inside the backward scan and to edge-position
lookup. The backward scan now reuses the current edge position directly for
adjacency and removal marks. The parallel prototype clears certified pairs
before the serial pivot reduction, returns simplex locations, and is opt-in as
`alg=:parallel_cohomology`. On the smaller 512-point O(3) case at radius 1.6,
parallel cohomology took 31.2 ms and edge collapse took 18.9 ms. Relative
backend performance varies with the density, dimension and output requested.

## Mapper, kernels, inference and clustering

- **Mapper nerves.** All repeated eight-thread complete-cover runs returned
  4,560 edges, and simple, minimum-count, Jaccard and both percentage modes
  agree with serial intersection references. On the 512-set sparse cover,
  point memberships took 0.080 ms and 0.109 MiB, compared with 14.8 ms and
  83.9 MiB for pairwise intersections. Both returned 511 edges.
- **Radius graph selection.** The opt-in `radius_graph=:auto` chose indexing in
  10 of 48 cases. Every selected case beat its dense pairwise baseline in this
  sweep. On the 4,096-point, 4D case with 0.155% retained edges, construction
  took 7.47 ms and 4.37 MiB with auto selection, compared with 329 ms and
  257 MiB pairwise. Use `true` to select indexing explicitly and `false` for
  pairwise evaluation. At floating-point cutoffs, the indexed backend uses
  pointwise distance rounding.
- **Threaded Gram matrices.** The results matched serial entry for entry over
  15 ragged corpora per thread setting. At eight threads and a maximum of 16
  intervals, the smallest winning batch measured was 32 diagrams (0.26 vs
  0.58 ms). With a maximum of 80 intervals, eight diagrams already showed a
  small gain (0.59 vs 0.68 ms). For 48 diagrams and up to 80 intervals, the
  eight-thread time was 9.72 ms versus 29.8 ms serial.
- **CUDA scale-space prototype.** The RTX 4070 Laptop GPU matched the Float64
  CPU values across all ragged, empty and essential-interval cases
  (`rtol=1e-12`, `atol=1e-13`, largest absolute difference 1.38e-12).
  A 48-diagram batch with up to 80 intervals took 1.42 ms including transfers,
  plus 0.073 ms for packing. At eight diagrams with up to 16 intervals the
  GPU plus packing took 0.106 ms and the serial CPU took 0.032 ms. The largest
  corpus used 233,472 bytes of CuPy device-pool space. The CUDA code is an
  optional benchmark prototype.
- **CUDA apparent-pair discovery.** The separate kernels found the same
  cofacets as Julia for all 300,000 sampled Klein H₂ and O(3) H₃ columns.
  Warm end-to-end GPU times were 2.01 and 2.24 ms, including transfers.
  Eight-thread CPU discovery took 14.2 and 34.6 ms. The CuPy pools peaked
  at 5.53 and 9.72 MiB. The timings cover detection on already assembled
  columns.
- **Permutation replicates.** With 112 points in each group and 48
  permutations, 1, 2, 4 and 8 workers took 442, 272, 145 and 96.3 ms,
  respectively. Every run produced the same ordered null distribution
  and p-value of 1/49. Peak process RSS ranged from 760 to 815 MiB
  including imports and compilation. Each timed call allocated about 187 MiB
  over its lifetime.
- **ToMATo merges.** The 32,768-vertex path performed 16,383 accepted merges
  in 3.40 ms with union-find and 121 ms with whole-vector relabeling.
  Each path produced the same partition and merge records. Dedicated
  fixtures cover threshold equality, tied peak heights and multiway saddles.

## Package verification

Full suites passed on Julia 1.12.5 for TDARipserer, TDAPersistenceDiagrams,
TDAmapper, MetricSpaces, MultiparameterPersistence, PersistenceInference,
ToMATo and JuliaTDA. The umbrella and multiparameter suites also passed on
Julia 1.10.10. TDAmapper, PersistenceInference and ToMATo passed on Julia
1.9.4, and MetricSpaces passed on 1.8.5. Julia 1.6.7 passed the Rips and
cubical backend tests, zigzag oracle and Gram-matrix tests. The umbrella and ToMATo documentation builds executed their worked
examples. The smoke test rendered Mapper and persistence PNGs.

The full umbrella suite with eight threads exposed stale MetricSpaces calls
to a removed thread helper. The distance, neighborhood and density paths now
use Julia's thread scheduler. The local lockfiles for MetricSpaces, TDAmapper
and ToMATo were refreshed to resolve the updated MetricSpaces dependencies
and the AdaMapper extension. The rendered smoke run used the docs environment
with CairoMakie. An initial run emitted a transitive GeometryBasics
precompilation diagnostic and passed all seven assertions. A rerun with
`--compiled-modules=existing` passed cleanly.
